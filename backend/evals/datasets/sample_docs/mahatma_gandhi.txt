Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, widely known as Mahatma Gandhi, was an Indian lawyer, anti-colonial nationalist, and political ethicist who employed nonviolent resistance to lead the successful campaign for India's independence from British rule. He was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, a coastal town in present-day Gujarat, and assassinated on 30 January 1948 in New Delhi.

Gandhi studied law at the Inner Temple in London and was called to the bar in 1891. In 1893 he travelled to South Africa to represent an Indian merchant in a legal dispute and ended up staying for over two decades. There he led the Indian community in protests against discriminatory laws and developed the political philosophy he called satyagraha, often translated as truth-force or soul-force, which centered on nonviolent civil disobedience.

After returning to India in 1915, Gandhi joined the Indian National Congress and gradually became its most influential leader. He led major campaigns against British rule, most famously the 1930 Salt March, a 240-mile walk from Sabarmati Ashram to the coastal village of Dandi to protest the British salt tax. The march was a turning point that drew worldwide attention to the Indian independence movement.

During the Second World War, Gandhi launched the Quit India Movement in 1942, calling for an immediate British withdrawal from India. He was imprisoned along with most of the Congress leadership. India eventually gained independence on 15 August 1947, though the country was partitioned into India and Pakistan, an event accompanied by widespread communal violence that deeply distressed Gandhi.

Gandhi advocated for religious pluralism, the upliftment of the so-called untouchables whom he called Harijans, and economic self-reliance symbolized by the spinning wheel, or charkha, which appeared on the original flag of the Indian National Congress. He lived simply, wore homespun cloth, and undertook several hunger strikes to press political and moral demands.

His birthday, 2 October, is observed in India as Gandhi Jayanti and worldwide as the International Day of Non-Violence. Gandhi's methods inspired civil rights and freedom movements globally, including those led by Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.
